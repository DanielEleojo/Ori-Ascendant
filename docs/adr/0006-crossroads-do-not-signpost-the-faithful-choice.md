# Crossroads do not signpost the faithful choice — discernment is the test

At a Crossroads the option that keeps faith with the life's vowed Ori is **never
labelled and never obvious**. The player has to *recognise* which choice embodies
their virtue by reading the situation — there is no flagged "correct" answer, no
"this one = Mercy" hint. Spotting the faithful path is the skill the Crossroads
tests; the player learns whether they held only afterward, as the steadfastness
tally ("held N of M") moves.

This sits on top of two earlier rules and sharpens them. It keeps **"temptation,
not a trap"**: the faithful option always *exists* on the table, so a steadfast life
is always achievable — what is hard is *discerning* it, not *finding* it. And it
extends **ADR-0004** (the Crossing is a transparent steadfastness test): the *odds*
and the *running tally* are always shown, but the per-choice alignment is **not**
shown before you choose — transparency is about the test's fairness and the
outcome, not about pre-labelling each option.

The trade-off is real. Obvious, labelled choices would be accessible and
frustration-free, but shallow — a pick-your-virtue button drains the meaning from
steadfastness and from the Chronicle. Hidden choices make steadfastness *earned*
(you must understand your virtue well enough to live it), make a fallen Nickname
land (the Chronicle can reveal a straying the player didn't realise they made), and
fit a cosmology where an Ori is lived through discernment. The cost is that the deck
is harder to author *fairly*: a faithful path that is hidden must still be
**discernible on reflection**, never an arbitrary guess. The pre-launch §7.10
native-speaker / community review is where that fairness is checked.

## Consequences
- The authored Crossroads deck (slice #10) must embed each option's virtue **inside
  the action**, never announce it. The §7.10 review explicitly checks that the
  faithful path is discernible-but-not-obvious — meaningful, not a coin-toss.
- The Crossroads screen never labels options by virtue and never shows alignment
  before the choice (already true today); alignment surfaces only afterward, through
  the steadfastness tally.
- A fallen Cultivator's Nickname may name a stray the player did not intend — this
  is intended. The Chronicle records the path actually walked, not the one meant.
- The seed deck shipped before §7.10 is placeholder and is *not* expected to meet
  the discernment bar; meeting it is authoring work, gated on the review.
