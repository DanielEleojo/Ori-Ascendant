# The Crossing is a transparent test of steadfastness to your Ori

The Crossing's outcome (ascend vs fall) is no longer a flat 60/40 coin. It is a
function of **steadfastness** — how faithfully the player's choices held true to the
Ori they chose at the start of the life — and the steadfastness→chance reading is
shown to the player openly at all times. There is no right or wrong choice; the
test is coherence, not correctness.

The mapping is **probabilistic, not deterministic**: steadfastness dominates the
odds within a floor and a ceiling — a wavering life keeps a sliver of hope, a
steadfast one is never a certainty — so the river keeps the final word, fitting a
cosmology where an Ori is *chosen* yet still *tested* in àyé. The old flat 60% sits
near the curve's midpoint; the deity-Path stays orthogonal and never touches
Crossing odds (as in §2.3); steadfastness reads as a simple held-N-of-M rate.

This reopens two locked decisions. GAMEPLAY §2.3 fixed tribulation odds at a flat
60/40; that is retired. GAMEPLAY §8 vowed "no hidden dynamic odds, ever." We keep
that vow's *spirit* — the odds are dynamic but **never hidden** — and hold that this
is *more* honest than a coin flip: an earned, fully-disclosed outcome is less of a
slot machine than fixed RNG, not more. The one inviolable rule carried from §8: the
steadfastness→chance mapping is always on screen.

## Consequences
- `TribulationConfig.baseAscendChance` becomes a baseline/curve input, not the whole
  story; the steadfastness model and its on-screen display are new work.
- GAMEPLAY §8's honest-design commitments are amended, not abandoned — transparency
  is the binding constraint.
- Ori is promoted from "narrative anchor" to a mechanic (see CONTEXT.md: Ori,
  Steadfastness).
