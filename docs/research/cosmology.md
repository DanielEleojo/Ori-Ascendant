# Lens: cosmology

## Summary
The game's core metaphor maps remarkably well onto documented cosmology: àṣẹ is the Yoruba "power to make things happen," granted by Olódùmarè to all beings, and a person who learns to wield it is literally called an aláàṣẹ — so "cultivating àṣẹ" has an authentic anchor. The strongest find for the Tribulation is the Yoruba/Igala complex around death-as-homecoming: the proverb "Ayé l'ọjà, ọ̀run nilé" (the world is a marketplace, ọ̀run is home), the criteria for "good death" vs "bad death" that determine the quality of one's ancestorhood (mapping cleanly to the 60/40 ascend/fall with 1.0/0.4 multipliers), and the Igala belief that egwu (masquerades/the dead) return across the divide to bless the living. The Ancestral Council maps to the documented pattern where recent named ancestors are venerated individually while older ones merge into a collective lineage blessing — exactly the council-of-5 retiring into permanentAseBonus. Proposed stage ladder uses generic, non-initiatory Yoruba descriptors (Ọmọ Ayé → Akẹ́kọ̀ọ́ → Awo → Aláàṣẹ → Àgbà → Aṣẹ́gun) across tiers Ayé and Ọ̀run, deliberately avoiding real priestly offices (Babaláwo, Olúwo, Ìyánífá) and the supreme God names (Olódùmarè/Ọlọ́run, Igala Ọjọ́), which are the main red lines along with egungun regalia, Ifá liturgy, and witchcraft tropes. One practical flag: the placeholder "Ọmọ Ane" collides with the Ane path name and mixes Yoruba ọmọ with Igala Anẹ (the consistent Igala form would be Ọ́ma Anẹ̀); renaming stage 1 to "Ọmọ Ayé" resolves both issues.

## Recommendations

### [high] Stage names: generic Yoruba descriptors, not priestly titles
Use the 6-stage ladder Ọmọ Ayé → Akẹ́kọ̀ọ́ → Awo → Aláàṣẹ → Àgbà → Aṣẹ́gun (full table in numbers section). Every word is common Yoruba vocabulary describing a person's standing (child, learner, initiate, wielder-of-àṣẹ, elder, victor) rather than a conferred religious office. 'Awo' at stage index 2 is deliberate: itefa initiation is the moment a Yoruba initiate enters the mysteries and their path is fixed — which is exactly where the game's path choice (Ane/Sango/Osun) unlocks. 'Aláàṣẹ' is directly attested in scholarship as 'a person who learns to use the essential life force to willfully effect change' — the perfect Tier-1 entry stage for an àṣẹ-cultivation game.

### [high] Tier names: Ayé (the marketplace) and Ọ̀run (the road home)
Tier 0 = Ayé, Tier 1 = Ọ̀run, mirroring the core Yoruba cosmological pair: the visible world and the invisible otherworld that coexist in the same space. Subtitle them 'The Marketplace' and 'The Road Home' after the proverb 'Ayé l'ọjà, ọ̀run nilé.' Do NOT call tiers 'Path of Earth/Sky' — 'path' is already the gameplay term for Ane/Sango/Osun and would collide. Tier-1 framing: the cultivator is still alive but now 'ọ̀run-facing,' walking toward the crossing.

### [high] Tribulation = Ìrékọjá, 'The Crossing' — death-as-homecoming, not divine judgment
There is an authentic concept that fits exactly: in Yoruba and Igala thought, the death of a fulfilled elder is a celebrated homecoming across the divide between ayé and ọ̀run (river-crossing imagery is widespread; Igala egwu masquerades ARE the dead returning across it). Crucially, the tradition distinguishes 'good death' (aged, fulfilled destiny, befitting rites → full venerated ancestor) from lesser deaths — which maps 1:1 onto ascend (bonusMultiplier 1.0) vs fall (0.4). Frame the 60/40 roll as 'how truly the orí you chose before birth holds at the river,' not as judgment by a named deity. Visual: a night river, drums and lights of the lineage on the far bank. The 'a fallen cultivator still produces an ancestor' business rule is itself authentic — the lineage performs rites so that no forebear is lost (cf. Igbo ikwa ozu, where rites are what secure the dead their place among ancestors). Never depict Olódùmarè/Ọlọ́run or Igala Ọjọ́ as the judge, gatekeeper, or any rendered entity.

### [high] Ancestral Council mechanics already match documented veneration structure — say so in flavor
Sources confirm: recent, named ancestors are venerated individually (shrine, offerings, the family Alàgbà presiding), while older ancestors gradually merge into a collective lineage blessing. That is literally the game's design: 5 named council seats (active 0.25 × bonusMultiplier each) and retirement baking into lineage.permanentAseBonus. Flavor the retirement as 'settling into the foundation of the house' — the ancestor is not deleted, they become the ground the lineage stands on. Flavor the council screen as a lineage shrine (carved staffs, calabash, flame motifs), explicitly NOT as egungun masks (see red lines). Generation++ flavor bonus: the Yoruba belief in àtúnwá (lineage rebirth — names like Babatunde 'father returns', Yetunde 'mother returns') authentically frames each new generation as the lineage returning stronger.

### [high] Cultural red lines — what would read as disrespect
(1) Never render, name, or stat-ify the supreme God (Olódùmarè/Ọlọ́run, Igala Ọjọ́) — the traditions themselves do not depict Him iconographically. (2) Never use real initiatory offices as level names or cosmetics: Babaláwo, Ìyánífá, Olúwo, Ẹlẹ́gùn, Alagbaa are earned ranks in a living religion (Lucumí practitioners actively police non-initiate use). (3) No egungun masks/regalia as costumes or ancestor portraits — rights to egungun cloth are hereditary and sacred; depict ancestors abstractly (light, river, flame, stars). (4) No reproduction of actual liturgy: no Odù Ifá verses, no 16-cowrie/ikin divination as a game mechanic, no bàtá rhythms as 'spells,' no depiction of the igbodu interior (initiation is closed). (5) Orisha are patrons who bless, never collectibles, pets, or boss fights; Sango and Osun flavor text should invoke documented attributes (Sango: thunder, double axe, justice, royal Oyo ancestor; Osun: river, sweetness, fertility, protector of humanity) without marriage-drama tropes. (6) Zero 'voodoo'/witchcraft framing: no Ìyàmi/àjẹ́ content, no skulls/dark-sacrifice cliches; do not gamify ẹbọ (offering/sacrifice). (7) Don't homogenize: Ane/Anẹ is the Igala earth deity, distinct from Igbo Ala and Yoruba Ilẹ̀ — label each path's tradition of origin in flavor text, and ship an in-game glossary/'About' screen stating the game is a fantasy homage by a descendant drawing on Igala, Yoruba, and Igbo traditions (the appropriation literature's test is consent, context, compensation, and centering custodians — a heritage statement plus at least one native-speaker/community review pass before launch satisfies the solo-dev version of this). (8) Keeping xianxia loanwords ('cultivator', 'tribulation') is fine — they signal genre fantasy, which actually helps keep distance from claiming to depict the real religion.

### [medium] Rename stage 0 from 'Ọmọ Ane' to 'Ọmọ Ayé' (collision + language-mixing fix)
The placeholder 'Ọmọ Ane' has two problems: (a) UX — stages 0–2 are explicitly path-less, yet the stage-0 name contains the Ane path's name, implying an affiliation that doesn't exist yet; (b) linguistic — it mixes Yoruba ọmọ with Igala Anẹ; the consistent Igala form is Ọ́ma Anẹ̀ (Wiktionary confirms Igala ọ́ma 'child', cognate of Yoruba ọmọ via Proto-Yoruboid). Recommended: 'Ọmọ Ayé' (Child of the World), which also rhymes with the Tier-0 name. If the dev wants the Igala earth anchor at stage 0 anyway, use the all-Igala 'Ọ́ma Anẹ̀' and accept the path-name overlap consciously.

### [high] Practical: Yoruba diacritics need early font testing in Unity
Correct orthography is itself a respect issue (tone marks change meaning: àṣẹ 'power' vs aṣọ 'cloth' class of errors). Display names use subdot characters (ẹ, ọ, ṣ) plus combining tone marks (ọ́, ẹ̀, ọ̀ọ́). Test TextMeshPro rendering of 'Akẹ́kọ̀ọ́' and 'Aláàṣẹ' in week 1 — most default fonts fail on subdot+tone stacking; Noto Sans (or Noto Sans Yoruba coverage) as TMP fallback font solves it. If a font path truly can't render combining marks, degrade to dotted-letters-only (Akẹkọọ, Alaaṣẹ) rather than stripping to ASCII, and keep full diacritics in the glossary screen. Per CLAUDE.md these names live in /Resources/StageConfigs/ assets, so the rename is config-only.

### [medium] Orí framing for the title loop
The game title 'Ori Ascendant' is well-grounded: orí (the 'inner head', orí-inú) is chosen kneeling before Olódùmarè pre-birth (àkúnlẹ̀yàn) and is the personal destiny one must align with through effort — an idle-game progression metaphor that requires zero distortion. Use it for the tribulation roll ('your orí holds true / your orí falters at the river') and for the welcome-back screen ('your orí kept watch while you were away' for offline progress). Avoid depicting the pre-birth choosing scene itself (Ajalá's workshop) as a literal game scene — reference it only in text.
